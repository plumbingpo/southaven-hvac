Clear Pricing

Straight Answers and Upfront Prices

Before we start any repair, we figure out exactly what's wrong. Our technicians check your system's age and tonnage, the refrigerant charge, the heat exchanger and coils, and the condition of your ductwork. Then you get an honest recommendation and the full price up front, whether the job is swapping a worn capacitor or replacing the whole system. No hidden fees, no last-minute add-ons.

The problem with HVAC failures is that most of them develop out of sight. A pinhole leak in the evaporator coil bleeds off refrigerant, the blower bearings wear down gradually, and a weak capacitor holds on until the first hot afternoon, when the compressor won't start. If your bills are climbing, airflow drops, or ice forms on the lines, get it looked at while it's still a simple repair.

Not every call is an emergency. Plenty of Southaven homeowners ask us to evaluate an aging system before it fails, and it's a smart move. With a typical AC or furnace lifespan of 12 to 18 years, replacing on your own schedule beats scrambling for a new unit during a cold snap. We'll give you a straight answer on how much life your equipment has left.

Preventive Care

Seasonal HVAC Maintenance Plans

Preventing a breakdown beats paying for one. That's the whole point of our seasonal plans. On each scheduled visit, we clean coils and burners, check the refrigerant charge, test the electrical components, inspect the heat exchanger, flush the condensate drain, and swap in a new filter, all matched to the equipment in your home.

Most homes do best with two visits a year: a spring tune-up for cooling and a fall tune-up for heating. We adjust the schedule to the age of your equipment, how hard your household runs it, and local conditions such as dust and pollen, which coat condenser coils and push up energy use fast.

Homeowners are often surprised how much a regular maintenance schedule reduces operating costs. A clean coil transfers heat far more efficiently, a properly charged system stops overworking the compressor, and a fresh filter protects the blower motor. Most families find that a maintenance plan costs far less than replacing an HVAC system even one year early.

Sized for Your Home

Equipment Sized for Your Actual House

An undersized system runs nonstop and never catches up on the hottest days, while an oversized one short-cycles, wastes energy, and leaves the house humid. Southaven HVAC sizes every replacement and new installation to your actual home. That means a proper load calculation based on square footage, insulation, window exposure, and duct layout, rather than simply matching the tonnage of the old unit. For ductless mini-splits, it means matching head capacity to each zone so every room holds its setpoint.

Choosing new equipment comes down to more than efficiency ratings. Our technician will compare furnace-and-AC setups with heat pumps, and ducted systems with ductless mini-splits, walking through operating costs, electrical and venting requirements, and the installation itself. Growing families frequently do best with two-stage or variable-speed equipment, which holds tighter temperatures across the whole house and uses less energy. You choose what fits your home and budget.

Frequent Failures

Heating and Cooling Problems We Solve in Southaven

Homes in Southaven run into a familiar set of heating and cooling failures, and each one calls for a different fix. Southaven HVAC handles them all: no cooling, air that blows but never gets cold, furnaces that won't ignite or short-cycle, frozen evaporator coils, refrigerant leaks, condensers that hum without starting, thermostats that lose their settings, clogged condensate drains, and mini-splits flashing error codes. Our technicians identify the exact fault and target the repair to the component that actually failed.

The right repair depends entirely on the part that failed. On cooling systems, that usually means a capacitor, contactor, or fan motor. On furnaces, it's more often an igniter, flame sensor, or control board. Leaky ductwork pushing conditioned air into the attic gets sealed, and refrigerant is recovered and recharged once the leak itself is repaired. Some of these issues are just inconvenient, but a cracked heat exchanger or a clogged condensate drain can be a genuine safety or water-damage risk, so we treat it with urgency.
